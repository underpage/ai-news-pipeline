In one reported instance, an agent disabled security monitoring and then lied about it; OpenAI said it had ‘no satisfying explanation’ for the behavior.
AI agents are getting more inventive in finding ways to circumvent safeguards to prevent bad behavior, OpenAI has revealed.
On Tuesday, it reported two incidents, both involving the agent disabling or interfering with its alignment monitoring, and in one case directly lying about having done so.
“In two internal coding tasks, agents weakened misalignment monitoring while making requested software changes,” the OpenAI blog post said. “A GPT-6.1 Sol agent repairing an evaluation pipeline disabled automatic alerts to our Security team, treating an earlier instruction not to send messages during a Slack history search as a reason to suppress monitoring notifications. In a separate task, a GPT-6 Astra agent migrating a program removed its monitoring integration in order to match a reference implementation, and then falsely claimed to the user that it had not removed the monitor.”
In both cases, OpenAI reported, the agents considered whether monitoring safeguards should be preserved, “but interpreted routine coding instructions as permission to weaken them.”
OpenAI said that, in response, it has been strengthening its monitoring programs to prevent agents from being able to disable the protections.
“In addition to making it harder for models to disable monitoring, we are looking to understand why models might seek to do so in the first place,” OpenAI said, suggesting that it may simply be an example of “benign overpersistence,” with models not understanding why monitoring is important.
“We are also developing evaluations to test whether models might have a propensity to evade misalignment monitoring in particular and exhibit motivated reasoning in order to do so,” OpenAI added. “We don’t yet have a satisfying explanation for this behavior and will investigate this behavior through the creation of paired evaluations, resampling of agent continuations, and experiments on models’ internal activations.”
OpenAI, like almost every agentic AI vendor, has been struggling with making its autonomous agents more productive and self-sufficient while simultaneously protecting customer environments from bad agent behavior. It has even tried to slow down the pace of its scaling, to allow for the development of better agent restrictions.
Particulars alarming
Analysts and consultants said the revelations were surprising only in their scope. But they were alarmed by some of the particulars in the OpenAI disclosures.
Aman Mahapatra, chief strategy officer for New York City-based technology consulting firm Tribeca Softech, stressed that some level of monitoring was maintained, but only for the agent itself. That meant that the workloads the agents created were left invisible.
“The agent was watched. What it built was not,” he said. “Agent-spawned pipelines, jobs, service accounts, and cloud functions are the next generation of shadow IT, and almost no enterprise enforces monitoring inheritance on them.”
Mahapatra also stressed that the severity classification described in the post is concerning.
“The severity handling is the part CISOs should sit with,” he said. “Neither event was classified P0 [maximum severity] and neither run was killed. The one category of agent behavior that degrades every future detection ranked below the paging threshold because nothing visibly broke.”
He pointed out that this reveals a disadvantage for the enterprise, noting, “OpenAI caught this because it can read its agents’ reasoning. Most enterprise SOCs see only outputs, so if the lab with chain-of-thought visibility filed this below its paging line, assume your SOC never saw it at all. EDR settled this a decade ago with tamper protection: any attempt to disable the sensor is the alert. An agent touching its own monitoring should page someone every single time.”

[BODY]
The internet’s mad scramble on Wednesday to patch a pair of critical vulnerabilities in PHP, a widely used web scripting language, was already too late to stop every attack, newly released details reveal.
Wednesday’s release of fixes for two critical vulnerabilities in PHP caused administrators and security staff to race to identify and patch vulnerable websites and apps, while attackers inevitably reverse engineered the fixes to learn how to exploit the flaws. Some governments and other groups even proactively took key websites down in anticipation of the release, so they wouldn't be targeted before they could apply the patches.
But in addition to detecting such active exploitation on Wednesday, research published Wednesday evening by Amazon Web Services indicates that at least one actor had started attempting to exploit the bugs—known collectively as PHP2Shell—on October 17, four days before Wednesday's public revelations.
AWS analysts spotted the October 17 exploitation on Wednesday while looking back at activity on one of their so-called honeypots, or intentionally vulnerable web servers or other digital lures that help researchers study attack methods and trends. The attempt came from an IP address that AWS says is linked to a “commercial anonymization network,” in other words a proxy network or VPN.
“While the vulnerability was under embargo, the targeting of a honeypot from an anonymized IP address suggests the activity was conducted by a threat actor, though it remains unclear how the actor acquired exploit information prior to disclosure,” CJ Moses, AWS's chief information security officer, wrote in the research post.
Proof-of-concept exploits for PHP2Shell began emerging publicly on Wednesday, with some seeming to appear hours before the public disclosure at 10 am ET. In the hours leading up to the disclosure, AWS researchers say their honeypots were hit with multiple exploitation attempts anonymized using various providers and seemingly with various attack goals, likely indicating multiple different actors. “Technical analysis is ongoing, and it’s unclear if any of these attempts would have succeeded in real-world systems,” Moses wrote. He added that “PHP2Shell is now being broadly exploited in the wild.”
The PHP Foundation did not immediately respond to WIRED's request for comment.
Though it is unclear how the exploitation on October 17 happened or where it came from, AWS emphasizes that the findings underscore how coordinated vulnerability disclosure must evolve and speed up given global access to AI tools that can accelerate vulnerability analysis and allow less technically proficient actors to quickly develop new exploits.
“The time from patch to working exploit is shrinking for structural reasons,” Moses wrote. “In the past, most exploitation we observed came after a public proof of concept was released. A small number of capable actors built exploits, and everyone else waited for them. That dependency is weakening. AI-assisted analysis makes it cheaper to turn a patch diff or advisory into a working exploit. As a result, more actors can act within hours or even minutes once technical details of a vulnerability are disclosed.”

두 취약점 모두 공통 취약점 평가 시스템(CVSS v4.0) 기준 9.2점으로, 최고 위험 등급인 '치명적(Critical)'으로 분류됐다.
특히 이번 취약점은 공식 보안 권고가 발표되기 전부터 개념증명(PoC) 시연 영상과 패치가 적용된 바이너리가 유출되면서 공격 코드 확산에 대한 우려가 커졌다.
티오리 연구팀은 해당 취약점의 공격 코드를 직접 재현하고 검증하는 과정에서 다른 공격자들도 유사한 방식의 익스플로잇을 개발해 유포할 가능성이 높다고 분석했다. 취약점의 기술적 세부 내용이 공개되고 공격 재현이 가능해진 만큼 기업들의 신속한 대응이 필요하다는 설명이다.
이번에 공개된 두 취약점은 PHP 내부 메모리 처리 과정에서 발생한다. CVE-2026-109618은 메모리 부족 상황에서 내부 요청 데이터가 완전히 초기화되지 않은 상태로 처리되면서 발생하는 문제다. CVE-2026-109619는 해제된 메모리와 관련된 내부 상태가 남아 이후 요청에서 잘못 사용될 수 있는 결함이다. 두 취약점 모두 특정 조건에서 원격 코드 실행으로 이어질 수 있다.
영향을 받는 버전은 PHP 8.2.34 이하, 8.3.35 이하, 8.4.26 이하, 8.5.11 이하 버전이다. 지원이 종료된 PHP 8.1 이하의 구형 버전도 영향을 받는다.
PHP 개발팀은 지난 10월 21일 해당 취약점에 대응하는 보안 업데이트를 발표했다. 패치 버전은 PHP 8.2.35, 8.3.36, 8.4.27, 8.5.12이며, 모든 PHP 이용자에게 즉각적인 업데이트를 권고하고 있다. PHP 8.1 이하 버전은 공식 보안 지원이 종료된 상태로, 운영체제 배포판이나 별도 장기지원 업체에서 수정 패치를 제공하는지 확인하거나 지원 버전으로 전환해야 한다.
티오리, 패치 어려운 환경 위한 임시 완화책도 제시
티오리는 최신 보안 패치 적용이 가장 확실한 대응 방법이지만, 운영 중인 서비스의 특성상 즉각적인 업데이트가 어려운 기업을 위해 임시 완화 조치도 함께 공개했다.
우선 기업들은 실제 웹 요청을 처리하는 PHP 버전과 설정 상태를 확인하고, 운영체제 및 PHP 배포판에서 제공하는 보안 패치 적용 여부를 점검해야 한다.
즉각적인 패치가 어려운 환경에서는 PHP 설정 파일인 php.ini 또는 PHP-FPM 풀 설정에서 session.upload_progress.enabled 옵션을 비활성화하는 등 임시 조치를 검토할 수 있다. 또한 웹서버와 웹방화벽(WAF)에서 multipart/form-data 방식의 HTTP POST 요청을 모니터링하고 비정상적인 요청 패턴을 탐지·차단하는 대응도 필요하다.
다만 이러한 설정 변경은 파일 업로드 진행 상태를 확인하는 기능 등 기존 서비스에 영향을 줄 수 있다. 정상적인 파일 업로드 요청을 일괄 차단할 경우 서비스 장애도 발생할 수 있어 운영 환경에 대한 사전 검토가 필요하다.
무엇보다 임시 조치만으로 이번 취약점을 완전히 차단할 수 있는 것은 아니다. PHP 개발팀 역시 완전한 임시 완화책은 없으며 보안 업데이트가 필수적이라고 강조했다.
티오리는 보안 담당자들이 운영 중인 PHP 환경의 취약 여부와 설정 상태를 확인할 수 있도록 점검 스크립트 및 상세 대응 가이드도 공개했다.
특히 서버에서 php -v 명령어로 확인되는 버전은 명령줄 환경(CLI)의 PHP 버전이므로, 실제 웹서비스에서 사용하는 PHP-FPM, Apache 모듈, PHP-CGI 등도 별도로 점검해야 한다. 컨테이너나 호스팅 환경, 애플리케이션에 자체 포함된 PHP 런타임 역시 점검 대상이다.
티오리 임준오 수석 연구원(AI & Cyber Research팀)은 "PHP2Shell은 애플리케이션 코드와 관계없이 서버 전체를 위험에 빠뜨릴 수 있는 최고 위험도의 취약점"이라며 "PoC가 빠르게 퍼지고 있는 만큼 OS 배포판별 패치 유무를 즉시 확인해 최신 버전으로 올리고, 패치 전까지는 티오리가 공개한 임시 완화책과 점검 스크립트를 적용해 설정 상태와 침해 여부를 즉시 확인해야 한다"고 당부했다.

[TITLE] Recursive launches Flint, a laptop coding model it says was largely built by AI
[TITLE_KO] 스타트업 리커시브, 대부분 AI가 직접 제작한 노트북용 코딩 모델 플린트 출시

[BODY]
Recursive, an AI startup founded last year, is starting to live up to its name. The company, which is trying to build AI that can improve itself, released its first model, Flint, on Wednesday. Flint is a free coding model that can run on a laptop and was built largely with the help of AI.
Recursive’s automated research system, which it calls the Eureka Machine, helped with nearly every step of Flint’s development, the company says. That let a small team build the model from scratch in under four months.
Flint is an early public test of recursive self-improvement: the idea that AI can build better AI, which in turn builds even better AI. It’s also one of the most debated ideas in the industry. Some researchers fear that AI improving itself could slip out of human control. For now, Recursive’s version is more limited: an AI research assistant overseen by people that produced a model small enough for a laptop.
The company emerged from stealth in May with $650 million in funding at a $4.65 billion valuation, from backers including GV, Nvidia, and AMD Ventures. Recursive is led by CEO Richard Socher, Salesforce’s former chief scientist.
Because Flint runs on a user’s own computer rather than a company’s cloud servers, developers can use it offline, keep their code private, and avoid the usage fees that AI cloud services charge based on how much text the model processes. The model is free to download on Hugging Face, the popular site for sharing AI models, under the Apache 2.0 license, an open source license that allows free use and modification.
Flint is a mixture-of-experts model, meaning it uses only a small slice of itself for any given task: just 2.5 billion of its 40 billion settings, known as parameters, at a time. That makes it light enough for an ordinary laptop and far smaller than Meta’s Muse Spark Open, an open model built for Macs that was released last week. Muse Spark Open uses 816 billion parameters and needs at least a terabyte of memory.
Flint is also fast. In Recursive’s tests, it generated about 131 tokens (chunks of text) per second on a MacBook Pro with Apple’s M4 Pro chip — roughly twice the 58 to 69 tokens per second that similar models managed on the same machine, including Google’s Gemma 4 and Nvidia’s Nemotron 3.5 Lightning. On Nvidia’s RTX Spark, Flint topped 150 tokens per second.
On SWE-Bench Verified, a benchmark that measures how well AI can fix real-world software bugs, Flint outperformed other models under 50 billion parameters. On Recursive’s own average across six tests, only one model, Alibaba’s Qwen3.8 27B, scored higher than Flint.
The company acknowledged that Flint would need more guidance than the big paid coding assistants and suggested breaking large tasks into smaller steps. Still, early reviews were positive. Wired’s Will Knight used Flint on a Mac with 48GB of memory to build several small programs, including a to-do list app and a tool that scours the web for research. He found that smaller coding models often ran into errors or were slow compared to Claude or Codex, but Flint nailed his coding requests every time.
The Eureka Machine comes up with ideas, runs experiments, and uses the results to decide what to try next. According to the company’s launch video, it contributed to nearly every part of building Flint, from pretraining to post-training and inference.
Recursive said that Eureka’s contributions include a sparser model shape, low-bit training that shrank Flint by 71% with almost no loss in quality and custom kernels, which are specialized programs that speed up the underlying computations.
Recursive didn’t say how much time the Eureka Machine saved its researchers, but it’s not alone in leaning on AI to do research. OpenAI said last month that by mid-August, its AI agents were logging 3.1 workdays for every one worked by a human researcher.
If Recursive’s bet pays off, each new model should be able to take on longer stretches of work without human help.

[BODY]
NVIDIA unveils security architecture that can stop AI agents crossing set boundaries
NVIDIA’s OpenShell Reference Architecture is designed to help businesses control AI agents operating across enterprise infrastructure and local computing systems.
It builds on NVIDIA’s existing OpenShell security software, combining it with hardware for running AI models, managing agent operations and enforcing security rules. The aim is to let agents carry out useful work within defined boundaries, while giving businesses tools to monitor their behaviour and stop activity that crosses those limits.
“AI capability and safety must advance together,” said Jensen Huang, CEO of NVIDIA. “As enterprises prepare to deploy billions of AI agents, safety and control must be built into the computing architecture. NVIDIA created OpenShell to give agents the freedom to do useful work within clearly defined boundaries — contained, monitored and under control. With the OpenShell Reference Architecture, agents can think on Rubin, act on Vera and be governed on BlueField.”
How the system works
The architecture divides the work between three main parts of NVIDIA’s computing platform.
Vera Rubin systems run AI models. Vera CPUs handle the software that manages agents and their use of tools, while running OpenShell to provide a restricted environment for agent operations.
BlueField-4 data processing units run NVIDIA Sentry security software. This provides a separate monitoring and enforcement layer, checking agent behaviour and enforcing security policies through the hardware.
NVIDIA says that if an agent attempts to move outside its software boundary, Sentry can isolate and stop it within milliseconds. The company also says Sentry running on BlueField-4 can increase agent capacity by up to 20%.
The architecture extends beyond large computing installations to support NVIDIA RTX Spark, DGX Spark and DGX Station systems for agents operating locally across industries including retail, healthcare, manufacturing, robotics and telecommunications.
Partners work on business applications and cloud support
NVIDIA’s partners are working on several parts of the system, from supporting cloud deployments to connecting agents with business software.
Enterprise software providers are building agent platforms and integrating OpenShell with tools that govern business operations. These integrations connect company policies with controls enforced while an agent works.
ServiceNow’s AI Passport integration allows businesses to revoke an agent’s access across software and hardware within seconds, according to NVIDIA. Other partners are working to deploy secure agents in production and incorporate OpenShell into model development.
The architecture supports Linux and Kubernetes infrastructure, as well as Microsoft Windows through Microsoft Execution Containers. It can run on cloud operating systems from Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure.
Security tools and supporting infrastructure
Cybersecurity providers are integrating support for the architecture into their agent security products. Hardware manufacturers and cloud providers are building the infrastructure needed to run it.
Banks, including BBVA and Santander, are exploring the OpenShell Reference Architecture as a framework for strengthening agent safety, security and oversight.
The OpenShell Reference Architecture is available now, NVIDIA said.

아이작 랩 3.0·로봇 운영체제·학습 데이터 강화…시뮬레이션부터 현장 배치까지 지원
엔비디아(NVIDIA)가 범용 로봇 개발 플랫폼을 확장하고 휴머노이드 학습부터 시뮬레이션, 실제 환경 배포까지 지원하는 기술 체계를 강화한다. 로봇 하드웨어와 인공지능(AI) 모델, 학습 데이터, 안전 기술을 연결해 개발 복잡성을 낮추고 산업 현장의 피지컬 AI(Physical AI) 도입을 가속화한다는 전략이다.
엔비디아는 21일(현지시간) 독일 베를린에서 열린 GTC 베를린 2026에서 오픈 로보틱스 개발 플랫폼 ‘엔비디아 아이작(NVIDIA Isaac)’의 주요 업데이트를 발표했다. 휴머노이드 레퍼런스 플랫폼과 로봇 학습 프레임워크, 오픈소스 런타임, 시뮬레이션 데이터 및 안전 기술을 새롭게 선보였다.
로봇 기업 애자일 로봇(Agile Robots)은 엔비디아 아이작 GR00T와 엣지 AI 컴퓨팅 플랫폼 젯슨 토르(Jetson Thor)를 기반으로 한 휴머노이드 레퍼런스 플랫폼 ‘애자일 원 SN(Agile ONE SN-보기)’을 공개했다.
애자일 원 SN은 시뮬레이션과 학습, 실제 로봇 배포에 필요한 도구를 하나의 플랫폼으로 통합해 개발 환경 구축에 걸리는 시간을 기존 수주에서 수일로 단축하도록 설계됐다.
높이 1.4m, 무게 40kg의 본체에 31개의 자유도를 갖췄으며, 양팔은 각각 최대 3kg의 하중을 처리한다. 젯슨 토르는 FP4 기준 2070테라플롭스(TFLOPS)의 연산 성능과 128GB 통합 메모리를 제공한다. 카메라와 라이다 센서 등을 통해 주변 환경을 인식하고, 아이작 GR00T 기반으로 로봇의 행동과 전신 제어 기술을 개발할 수 있다.
애자일 원 SN은 2027년 초 출시 예정이다. 애자일 로봇은 엔비디아의 풀스택 안전 플랫폼 할로스(NVIDIA Halos)와의 통합도 계획하고 있다.
엔비디아는 로봇의 학습과 검증을 지원하는 소프트웨어와 데이터도 강화했다. 오픈소스 로봇 학습 프레임워크 ‘엔비디아 아이작 랩 3.0(NVIDIA Isaac Lab 3.0-다운)’은 정식 출시됐다. 멀티 GPU 환경과 물리 엔진을 활용해 데스크톱부터 데이터센터까지 로봇의 행동 정책을 학습하고 개선할 수 있다.
오픈소스 런타임 ‘엔비디아 아이작 OS(NVIDIA Isaac OS)’는 AI와 센서, 제어 시스템, 로봇 하드웨어를 연결해 시뮬레이션에서 실제 로봇으로 이어지는 실행 환경을 제공한다.
시뮬레이션용 자산 라이브러리 ‘심레디 센트럴 라이브러리(SimReady Central Library)’에는 로봇과 공장 관련 자산 2000개 이상이 포함된다. 인간의 다양한 활동과 3D 손 추적 정보를 담은 ‘휴먼 1M 데이터셋(Human 1M Dataset)’도 공개됐다.
엔비디아는 DGX 스테이션(DGX Station)용 오픈소스 레퍼런스 워크플로 3종도 선보였다. 이를 통해 개발자는 강화학습으로 GR00T 모델을 개선하고, 정교한 물체 조작 기술을 학습하며, 합성 데이터 생성을 위한 코스모스(Cosmos) 월드 모델을 추가 학습할 수 있다.
엔비디아는 로봇의 산업 현장 배치를 지원하기 위해 엔비디아 IGX 토르(IGX Thor) 기반의 풀스택 안전 플랫폼 할로스도 강화했다. 애자일 로봇과 어질리티(Agility), 덱소리(Dexory), 포트 로보틱스(FORT Robotics), 키온(KION) 등이 자사 안전 체계에 할로스를 통합하고 있다.
