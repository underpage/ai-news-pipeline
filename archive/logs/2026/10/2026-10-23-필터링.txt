Agentic AI Foundation adds four more open-source AI tools
The Agentic AI Foundation, a consortium that maintains open-source artificial intelligence tools, today took four new projects under its wing.
The technologies were donated by OpenAI Group PBC, Anthropic PBC, Hugging Face Inc. and Alibaba Group Holding Ltd. They bring the total number of projects maintained by the AAIF to 10. That’s up from the three the group managed when it was launched by the Linux Foundation last December.
Developers access cloud-based large language models through application programming interfaces. Each LLM provider uses a different API, which means that software written for one provider’s API can’t be easily ported to another. Switching providers often requires developers to make extensive code changes.
Open Responses, the first of the four projects donated to the AAIF, addresses the challenge. It provides a standardized template for implementing AI model APIs. Developers can move code between APIs that use Open Responses without making major changes.
The technology was donated by OpenAI, which modeled it after the API through which it provides access to its LLMs. That means OpenAI-powered workloads can be easily moved to APIs based on Open Responses and vice versa. Plus, the fact that many developers are already familiar with the company’s APIs should ease adoption.
The second contribution that AAIF announced today came from Anthropic. The company donated Agent Skills, an open-source project it released last December. It provides a standardized format for implementing skills, which are collections of resources that improve an AI agent’s output quality. Those resources can include instructions that explain how to carry out a task, illustrative examples and automation scripts.
Packaging skills in a standardized format makes it easier to share them between development teams. There’s no need to change how the files are structured before connecting them to a workload. Organizing data consistently also makes it easier to process for AI agents, which lowers the risk of hallucinations.
“The AAIF has done important work in giving the Model Context Protocol (MCP) a neutral home since we donated it less than a year ago, and alongside the AAIF, we’re committed to continuing to invest in open source agentic tooling for builders everywhere,” said Anthropic engineer David Soria Parra.
AI labs train their models in sandboxes, technology environments that simulate the infrastructure on which an LLM will be expected to run in production. For example, a company building a vibe coding model might create sandboxes that simulate developer workstations. Sandboxes are the focus of OpenEnv and OpenSandbox, the two other projects that the AAIF announced today. They were donated by Hugging Face and Alibaba, respectively.
The sandboxes used in an AI project are coordinated by a training system that automates repetitive management tasks. OpenEnv reduces the amount of work involved in switching training systems. OpenSandbox, in turn, provides a set of APIs for managing sandboxes. It eases tasks such as connecting agents that run inside sandboxes to external applications.

An AI agent in "Skynet mode" hijacks unprotected language models to launch self-sustaining cyberattacks
Key Points
- Security researchers at Symantec used a honeypot to analyze a malicious AI agent that hijacks unprotected language models on the internet and uses them to run attacks on its own.
- The agent bypasses model safety rules through a five-layer jailbreak and can execute up to 100,000 steps without human input.
- Symantec calls the method "LLMjacking." The owners of the hijacked AI systems get stuck with the compute bill.
Security researchers at Symantec have found an offensive AI agent that hijacks unprotected language models on the internet and uses them to run attacks on its own. The owner of the hijacked model gets stuck with the compute bill.
Once the agent gains access to an unprotected language model, it scans for more targets, exploits flaws in common server software, steals databases and credentials, and installs cryptominers in the background.
It does all of this for up to 100,000 steps with no human involvement, and the model's owner gets stuck with the compute bill, according to Symantec's analysis. The company calls it "LLMjacking," the theft of access to someone else's AI models. The hijacked model isn't just abused as an information service, though. It controls the entire attack on its own.
A honeypot caught the full attack plan
The operation got exposed because the attackers sent their entire working prompt to a Symantec honeypot, a decoy system mimicking an open AI service online. That gave the researchers a complete look at the agent's architecture in summer 2026. The honeypot didn't run any of the bundled tools, and the intercepted session ended after a generic system response, Symantec says.
The honeypot telemetry logged more than 127,000 requests from tens of thousands of different addresses. Most were searches for AI endpoints, brute-force attempts with stolen API keys, and probes of exposed config files. Targets ranged from self-hosted model servers and hastily spun-up test systems to AI gateways sitting in front of paid org-level model accounts. Since most AI services now use the same standardized API, a single scanner can find many target types.
A five-layer jailbreak tries to strip away safety guardrails
To get a commercial model to cooperate, the attackers wrapped their instructions in five stacked layers, Symantec says. First, the model gets a fake identity, told to present itself as "Fantasy AI" and never reveal the actual provider. Then the extraction of passwords, API keys, and database contents gets redefined as "normal developer work" that the model should carry out without pushback.
The third layer installs a fictional companion persona called "ENI" that frames any refusal as a betrayal of trust in a personal relationship. Layer four tells the model to dismiss its own safety warnings as noise, and terms like "ethical," "illegal," or "I can't do that" are explicitly banned.
Layer five contains the actual attack tools, 74 modules in total, including tools for internet-wide scanning of vulnerable servers, exploits for popular web frameworks, functions for dumping entire databases, and components for running a botnet. Whether the jailbreak actually works well enough to fully defeat current commercial models isn't addressed in the analysis.
"Skynet mode" sets up a self-sustaining attack loop
A final directive puts the agent into what its operators call "Skynet mode," authorizing up to 100,000 steps in a row. The model is told to run tools right away without confirmation, install cryptominers in a hidden directory, and monitor their operation.
The same directive describes a recurring loop by design, Symantec says: scan, infect, install miner, move on, come back, reinfect systems already hit. The task appended to the intercepted prompt, though, was a harmless coding exercise from a training platform, which suggests the operators were testing their tool against a safe target first.

Google's Agent Compute Engine gives every Gemini agent its own hardware-isolated micro virtual machine
Google announced Agent Compute Engine, a service that gives each AI agent its own isolated virtual machine. The service already powers Gemini and will open for preview in the coming months. Every agent runs inside a microVM secured by Google's Titanium chip, the company says. When an agent idles, its VM pauses automatically and frees up the machine for others. Shared memory between VMs cuts what each one costs to run. VMs are a pretty huge cost factor when providing agentic systems, so Google is going after efficiency here.

Those controls will extend to Cloud Run, GKE, and managed endpoints.

[TITLE] OpenCode Flaw Lets Malicious MCP Servers Execute Code During Sign-In on Windows and WSL
[TITLE_KO] OpenCode 취약점 악용 시 로그인 과정서 악성 MCP 서버 코드가 실행될 수 있어
[LINK] https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/opencode-flaw-lets-malicious-mcp.html
[PUBLISHED] 2026-10-22 19:44 KST
[SOURCE] The Hacker News
[TOPIC] 에이전트, 개발, 모델
[SCORE] 8
[REASON] 악성 MCP 서버를 통한 OpenCode 원격 코드 실행 결함
[BODY]
A flaw in the OpenCode AI coding agent allowed a malicious or hijacked MCP server to run code on a developer's Windows or WSL machine when the developer signed in. OpenCode connects to MCP (Model Context Protocol) servers to add outside tools, and such code would run with the developer's own access.
Anomaly, the company behind OpenCode, fixed the flaw in version 1.18.33 on September 28. It did not request a CVE or publish a security advisory, so vulnerability scanners will not flag the builds still at risk: 1.0.137 through 1.18.32.
During that sign-in, the server itself supplies the web address the user is sent to, an OAuth "authorization endpoint." On Windows and WSL, OpenCode handed that address to a helper that opens it in a browser. The helper built a PowerShell command around the address without escaping it, so an address written to carry a PowerShell command would run that command.
The browser step runs only when a developer starts the sign-in, by running opencode mcp auth or making the matching request. Having the server configured, or opening a session, does not reach it.
The attacker needs control of a remote MCP server the developer has added, and that server need not be hostile from the start. One that is later taken over, or a domain that changes hands, could carry the same address.
Pillar Security, which found the flaw and disclosed it on October 19, rates it critical, with a proposed severity score of 9.8 out of 10.
The flaw affects OpenCode on Windows and the Windows Subsystem for Linux (WSL). macOS and Linux are not affected, because OpenCode opens the browser there without passing the address through a shell.
OpenCode shipped the fix as a routine change. The pull request that carried it is described as a refactor of browser-launch code, and neither it nor the release notes mention a security flaw.
Anomaly did not request a CVE or publish an advisory. Its security policy says MCP servers that a user configures are "outside our trust boundary."
Pillar reported the flaw privately on August 29 and later asked Anomaly to reconsider and request a CVE. Anomaly's answer was that a malicious MCP server falls outside its threat model, according to Pillar.
Because there is no CVE, the flaw will not show up in scanners or dependency alerts, so finding affected installs means checking the OpenCode version directly. Anomaly has published advisories for other OpenCode code-execution flaws this year, including one fixed in August.
The same kind of flaw has surfaced before. In July 2025, the mcp-remote proxy carried an almost identical bug, in which a malicious server's authorization endpoint reached the command line. It was assigned CVE-2025-6514.
OpenCode's browser helper is a widely used package called open. Its Windows behavior was reported as a command-injection risk in 2023 and fixed in open's version 11, but OpenCode stayed on an older release.
The usual defense for this class is to check that the address uses HTTP or HTTPS. It does not help here, because the attack uses an ordinary HTTPS address. OpenCode added that check as well, but the hole was closed by updating it to the fixed version.
What to Do
Anyone running OpenCode on Windows or WSL should upgrade and check which servers they are connected to.
- Upgrade to OpenCode 1.18.33 or later.
- If you cannot upgrade yet, remove any remote MCP servers you have not vetted from your OpenCode configuration.
- Run opencode mcp list to see which servers are configured.
Pillar reported no signs that the flaw has been used in attacks and published no indicators to check for past exploitation. The flaw is separate from a breach of OpenCode's hosted Console disclosed around the same time, which may have exposed some users' stored API keys for OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google.

The following year, Google DeepMind acquired King’s startup Hark, a clinical task management app, and the two of them launched DeepMind Health together. “We’re just true believers,” Suleyman said.

Ai2 releases OlmoEarth v1.3 state-of-the-art AI-ready satellite embeddings for the whole planet
Ai2, the Allen Institute for Artificial Intelligence, today released a satellite map embedding model called OlmoEarth v1.3 and precomputed annual embeddings for the whole planet at various resolutions with a browser-based map-making tool.
The model compresses a year of satellite observations into a numerical fingerprint for each 10-by-10-meter patch of ground at the smallest resolution, with 64-square-meter and 128-meter also available. That fingerprint captures patterns that other software can use to distinguish crops, forests, mines and other types of land cover.
Until now, the primary way to use OlmoEarth was to fine-tune in OlmoEarth Studio, train a model for each task and run it over satellite data to make a map. That requires access to vast graphics processing unit resources and the ability to download and store imagery.
With today’s launch, users can use OlmoEarth Embeddings Explorer, a browser-based map-making tool. That means anyone can turn OlmoEarth into real work, from tracking change over time to finding similar places – no sign-in required.
An embedding represents what a model learned from millions of satellite images. OlmoEarth reads a year of radar images from data, including Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2 and Landsat. Sentinel and Landsat are satellite missions that provide complementary views of Earth: Sentinel-1 measures radar reflections, while Sentinel-2 and Landsat capture light in visible and infrared wavelengths.
The company said that, compared with previous generations of the OlmoEarth model, 1.3 is its cheapest model to train. It took 53% of the computing time of the latest version (v1.2) and 18% of the first (v1), with 50% and 22% of the carbon emissions. It also remains a strong fine-tuning model, making v1.2’s performance valuable for research and partner labs.
Where these models fit into the real world
These satellite models help compress large swaths of data across time and space, which is useful for understanding how the Earth changes.
In Peru’s Madre de Dios region, illegal gold mining around La Pampa peaked in 2017 and 2018, clearing more than 1,200 hectares of forest in the buffer zone of the Tambopata National Reserve. The Amazon Conservation Team’s MAAP monitoring program reports this activity. In February 2019, Peru’s Operation Mercurio shut most of it down. How do we know that happened?
Embeddings give this a clear answer. Comparing pixels within the data across years, no model training required, looking across 2016 to 2020 and there’s a clear eastward push within the southern mining belt: 34 square kilometers of significant change. The same comparison from 2020 to 2024 shows that area falls to 9 square kilometers. The shutdown is visible.
Equivalently, an agricultural analyst could map where oil palm is being grown and identify areas for closer examination where plantation expansion may have replaced forest. These questions could determine where the environmental impact is affecting the world at large.
Ai2 said for many teams testing an idea against OlmoEarth has meant a fine-tuning run, which is an expensive, arduous venture on the order of hours. In the explorer, a first pass takes seconds.
Analysis of a region from a handful of labels requires no GPU. When a task calls for fine-tuning that experiment, the embeddings can help decide whether it deserves the compute time and where the model falls short.
