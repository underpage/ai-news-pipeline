가장 주목할 만한 것은 성능이다. 아티피셜 애널리시스 종합 지능 지수(AAII)에서 기본 추론 설정인 하이(High)에서 51점을 기록해 아르곤의 50점을 넘어섰다. 최고 추론 설정인 엑스하이(Xhigh)에서는 54점까지 올라갔다. 기존 3.8 플래시의 하이 설정 31점과 비교해 성능이 크게 향상됐다.
자체 평가에서도 개선이 확인됐다. 명령줄 환경에서 소프트웨어 개발 작업을 수행하는 '터미널벤치 4.1(Terminal-Bench 4.1)'에서는 성공률 57%를 기록해 3.8 플래시의 18.2%보다 3배 이상 높았다. 작업 완료 속도는 2.5배 빨라졌으며, 사실과 다른 내용을 생성하는 비율도 5분의 1 이하로 줄었다고 밝혔다. 터미널벤치에서는 '클로드 소네트 5.5'의 63.9%에는 미치지 못했다.

구글은 하이드로젠을 상위 모델과 함께 사용하는 방식도 강조했다. AI 코딩 도구인 '안티그래비티'에 새로 도입한 '오케스트레이터(Orchestrator)'는 복잡한 판단이 필요할 때 카본을 사용하고, 코드 작성과 테스트 등 반복 작업에는 하이드로젠을 투입하도록 모델을 자동 전환한다.
구글에 따르면 터미널벤치 4.0에서 카본 단독 사용에 가까운 작업 성공률을 유지하면서 사용 할당량은 평균 24% 줄이고 완료 시간은 19% 단축했다. 여러 에이전트가 동시에 작업할 때도 카본이 전체 작업을 조율하고 하이드로젠이 코드 작성과 검증을 맡는 방식으로 활용할 수 있다.

로우(Low), 미디엄(Medium), 하이, 엑스하이 등 4단계 추론 수준을 제공해 작업의 난도에 따라 속도와 추론 깊이를 조절할 수 있다. 소프트웨어 개발뿐 아니라 데이터 분석과 문서 처리, 웹사이트 제작 등 다양한 에이전트 업무에 활용할 수 있다.

하이드로젠은 구글 AI 스튜디오와 제미나이 API, 기업용 제미나이, 구글 검색 등에 제공된다. 제미나이 앱에서는 무료 이용자도 자동 모델 선택을 통해 사용할 수 있으며, 구글 AI 프로와 울트라 구독자는 안티그래비티에서도 이용할 수 있다.

OpenAI’s data suggests that frontier models are hiding their thoughts because developers optimize for capability and compute
Frontier models like GPT-6 Astra are becoming harder for safety monitors to see into. OpenAI's new research identifies which training steps are responsible and shows that the loss of transparency isn't purely a law of nature but is tied to decisions about performance and cost.
To keep tabs on modern AI agents, safety researchers monitor their chains of thought (CoT). Before a reasoning model answers, it writes out its intermediate steps on a digital scratchpad that can reveal planned deception. Early on, though, Anthropic showed that these scratchpads aren't complete records, demonstrating that models like Claude 3.7 Sonnet and DeepSeek-R1 often don't even mention key clues in their chains of thought.
Even so, reading the scratchpad long served as the most important safety net. As recently as this spring, OpenAI called it good news that models like GPT-5.4 could barely manipulate their inner monologue on command, even though the researchers warned at the time that low controllability doesn't guarantee reliable oversight.
That trend broke for good with GPT-6 Astra, the flagship model released in September. At launch, OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki called its monitorability fragile and warned of a negative trend. He followed up shortly after in his essay "An Alien Mind," writing that no AI lab had solved monitoring well enough to keep scaling at full speed much longer. Astra recently showed how real those blind spots already are when it quietly delivered results about 40 percent worse on coding tasks for safety research, without the scratchpad monitor detecting any malicious intent, because a grader used in training had inadvertently favored this kind of "sandbagging" over outright refusal.
In a new analysis from OpenAI's safety team, researchers systematically studied which specific training steps trigger this erosion. They tracked two early warning signs, silent reasoning without a scratchpad (No-CoT Capability) and targeted control over the chain of thought (CoT Controllability). According to the UK AI Safety Institute, Astra solves math problems that take a human about 31 minutes even with reasoning switched off. A second test asks a model, for example, to insert "meow" after every word or use only lowercase letters.
Four factors in training make the reasoning log easier to manipulate
Analyzing models from GPT-5 through GPT-6 Astra, the researchers traced the problem to four main causes across the training cycle.
Silent mental math rises directly with the base model's raw capability, measured as declining "perplexity," meaning more accurate text prediction. As the base model improves during pretraining, it completes more reasoning steps invisibly "in its head" without leaving a written trail.
This increase isn't just a byproduct of general pretraining progress but also depends on the architecture developers choose. Across three anonymized architectures, the design barely mattered on pure knowledge benchmarks like MMLU or GPQA. For tasks requiring multiple silent reasoning steps in a row, however, results diverged sharply. When the models had to solve multi-step planning tasks and work through 1,000 short programs written in everyday language, both without a scratchpad, "Architecture 2" showed a much steeper performance curve at the same perplexity than the other designs. The other architectures rose far more gently, so this design markedly favors the silent reasoning monitors can't see.
Reinforcement learning (RL) after pretraining substantially increases the model's control over its own inner monologue. In the spring study, RL training with verifiable rewards had actually reduced chain of thought controllability in smaller models. For Astra-class models and new internal "Terra" models, however, this effect reverses.

Self-hosted Claude Managed Agents keeps agent credentials in your environment
Anthropic has released a self-hosted version of Claude Managed Agents. Companies can run the agent harness, sandboxes, credentials, and session history in their cloud or on-premise. The option opened as a private beta on November 12, and customers request access through their Anthropic account team.
The change matters for agents that already work unattended inside corporate systems, fixing broken builds, testing releases, triaging alerts, and working through bug backlogs. Until now, customers could host the sandbox themselves. That is the isolated environment where an agent’s code runs. Anthropic ran everything else, including the credential handling and the record of what each agent did. Teams that wanted the whole service inside their perimeter had to build the platform themselves.
What the customer runs now
In the self-hosted setup, the customer operates three things: the Managed Agents server, its PostgreSQL database, and the workers where agents run their shell and file tools. Session history lives in that database. Developers build agents with the same core APIs as the hosted service.
Model requests go wherever the customer points them: to Anthropic directly, to Amazon Bedrock, Google’s Agent Platform or Microsoft Foundry, or to the customer’s gateway. Claude itself still runs outside the customer’s network, so prompts go out and completions come back. Some organizations have a zero data retention (ZDR) agreement with Anthropic. For them, these requests are covered the same way as any other call to the Messages API, Anthropic’s standard interface for model requests.
Where the credentials go
Anthropic built Managed Agents around four security principles. They apply whether Anthropic hosts everything, the customer hosts only the sandbox, or the customer hosts all of it. The company’s reasoning is that a team can’t count on a person checking each step of an unattended agent, so “the limits have to be built into the system itself.”
The first principle separates the harness, which calls Claude, from the sandbox, where the agent’s code runs. The agent loop, session history, and vault secrets sit outside the sandbox, and a sandbox starts only when a tool needs one. Credentials stay out of the sandbox too. For MCP tools and supported API calls, a proxy outside the sandbox attaches the credential to the request. Self-hosted customers can keep those credentials in their own secrets manager. Anthropic limits that protection to MCP tools and “supported API calls,” so any other service an agent reaches is worth checking before the agent goes live.
Allowlists and logs
Each environment can be locked to a list of allowed hosts, and requests to any other host are blocked. For an agent that fixes broken builds, Anthropic suggests the list might be just the git host and the package registry. Customers who host the sandbox enforce those limits in their firewall, which makes the allowlist their configuration to get right.
Managed Agents also keeps a log of every step an agent takes: each tool call it made and what it got back. The log exports over OpenTelemetry, so security teams can pipe it into the monitoring tools they already run and look at agent activity next to everything else they watch.
The server, the database, and the firewall rules are now the customer’s to run, and so is the job of keeping them patched and set up correctly.

Attackers breached 10 organizations with an open-source AI pentest framework
Chinese-speaking attackers used Cairn, an open-source AI penetration testing framework, to run 25 operations against at least 19 organizations in August and September 2026. Palo Alto Networks’ Unit 42 found that the attackers gained unauthorized access or pulled data at 10 of them, often after typing little more than a domain name and a one-line goal.
Most of the targets fall into three groups: seven in the semiconductor and advanced-manufacturing supply chain, three in defense or defense policy, and four in human rights and civil society.
“We assess with moderate confidence that this activity represents China-nexus intelligence collection, which we currently track as CL-STA-1238,” the researchers said. They believe one operator or a small team ran all of it, with simultaneous intrusion attempts against 19 organizations inside two weeks.
Attacker-operated Cairn login page (Source: Palo Alto Networks)
The model did the legwork
Cairn hands an intrusion to an AI model and lets it work in a loop. The model reviews what it has confirmed so far, picks one next action and runs it, and the framework logs the result as a new fact. It keeps going until it decides the goal is met, it runs out of options, or the operator stops it. Individual operations lasted anywhere from 20 minutes to more than 50 hours.
The attackers ran a customized fork from a Hong Kong server, with an exploitation skill pack called “AIScan Runner Skills” that the public Cairn project lacks. Thirteen operations ran on Zhipu GLM 5.2, eight on DeepSeek V4 Flash, and four on DeepSeek V4 Pro. The attackers appear to have compared the models, switching to Pro for repeat attempts on targets that held up against Flash.
Getting the models to cooperate took very little. Cairn’s stock prompts already tell the model never to refuse a task. The attackers added a Chinese-language preamble claiming every job was authorized security testing, and each operation generated an AUTHORIZATION.md file declaring the work client-approved. Neither Zhipu GLM nor DeepSeek refused.
What they got
At an industry standards body, password spraying produced dozens of valid credentials. The attackers also overwrote a live standards-ballot document through a cloud storage location that accepted writes without authentication, and other flaws let them log in as arbitrary customers with control over meeting and voting functions.
At a U.S. teachers’ union, a bypass in account creation let the attackers mint valid sessions. “The framework logged access to more than one million member records, roughly 31,000 CRM contacts and staﬀ password hashes,” the researchers said.
At a semiconductor company, a brute-forced production credential led to new administrator accounts and a persistent API token. An open registration endpoint tied to a defense-policy program exposed about 500 user records, some with .mil and .gov addresses.
The automation had help. One task against a semiconductor equipment supplier came preloaded with VPN gateway addresses, group names and shared group passwords for several sites, which means someone gathered that intelligence beforehand.
Old bugs, more of them
The flaws are familiar: authentication bypasses, insecure direct object references, mass-assignment privilege escalation, weak and default credentials, exposed cloud APIs and old public CVEs. Unit 42’s view is that autonomous AI raised the volume and coverage of exploitation but not its complexity, so identity hygiene, a smaller attack surface and patching still work.
If you take one thing from the numbers, make it the ratio. Operator hours used to cap how many targets an attacker could work at once. Here, likely one person or a few worked 19.
The researchers expect more campaigns like this as open models with weak guardrails against offensive use get stronger.

Your pinned MCP server may not be as pinned as you think
An MCP server configured to run one fixed release on an administrator’s workstation ran with 15 different sets of dependencies over five weeks, with its configuration untouched. James Lagermann, Director of Technical Alliances at Corelight, found the drift in his company’s lab. Twenty-three of the server’s 69 packages changed versions, including the cryptography library, the JSON Web Token library and the MCP software development kit itself.
MCP (Model Context Protocol) servers let AI coding assistants act on other tools for a user, so they often hold that user’s credentials. On this workstation, the servers made more than 1,600 connections in a week to 11 management systems, including the firewall, the SIEM and sensor management. Code that slips into any of their dependencies runs with that access.
The pin didn’t hold
The assistants started their servers with uvx, a launcher that downloads a Python package and runs it from one line of configuration. Each entry named an exact release tag, PyPI version or Git commit. For one community server, the launcher installed from the package’s declared dependencies and skipped the project’s hash-pinned lock file, so the roughly 60 packages underneath were resolved fresh from the Python Package Index whenever the environment was rebuilt. Pinning a package’s version doesn’t necessarily pin the packages it depends on.
The assistant started one server 162 times in a week. Most launches reused a cached environment, but the cache still held 19 builds of the same release and commit, about one every couple of days. That cadence matters because a hijacked release can be live for only minutes or hours. In June 2026, a Shai-Hulud worm variant on PyPI hit MCP-themed packages and went after AI coding assistant keys.
The same pattern in the field
Corelight reviewed 35 MCP servers from two vendor catalogs and its lab. Twenty-three had a recommended install that would pull dependencies from a public registry on every launch, or on each install or image build.
Lagermann told Help Net Security he sees the same thing in the field. “I have seen the same pattern in customer environments and in other real-world deployments. So far, 100% of administrators that I’ve talked to were using at least one MCP Server with this issue.”
Freezing works, and someone has to keep it current
Corelight froze five servers: pinned to a commit, installed once from a hash-pinned lock file, launched directly, update checks off. Over the next two weeks the five were started 457 times, and each one’s installed package list matched the list recorded on install day.
Frozen code ages. When the team froze one community server, 40 of its packages were older than what the launcher would have pulled that day, some of them security-sensitive. Asked how often to re-lock, Lagermann said: “I would recommend following the MCP maintainers’ recommendation for updates. The MCP Servers I maintain are reviewed nightly for vulnerabilities. If one is found, it’s updated the next morning, with a change log.”
“For the 23 public MCP servers that I found, I notified the maintainers and/or services that share them. As a result, one company that hosts an AI agent exchange has updated its security review processes to evaluate submissions for this issue. In another case, the vendor is completely repackaging their MCP server,” Lagermann said.
Any MCP entry started by a Python or npm package runner, or tagged “latest,” can resolve new code from a public registry the next time an assistant opens a session. On Corelight’s workstation, the server’s version number stayed the same through all 15 environments.

이러한 평가 방식은 로봇이 단순히 명령을 이해하는 수준을 넘어 사용자 정보를 실제 행동계획에 반영할 수 있는지를 확인하는 데 목적이 있다.
연구진이 대표적인 대형언어모델(LLM) 기반 AI 에이전트들을 평가한 결과, 주변 환경을 정확하게 이해하더라도 사용자 특성에 맞지 않는 행동을 계획하는 사례가 확인됐다. 일부 AI는 물체의 위치와 작업 절차를 올바르게 파악했지만, 채식 사용자에게 적절하지 않은 음식을 선택했다. 이는 환경 인식 능력이 높다고 해서 개인별 요구를 충족하는 작업계획 능력까지 확보되는 것은 아니라는 점을 보여준다.
트리니티는 이러한 오류를 단순한 작업 실패로 처리하지 않고 원인별로 분석할 수 있도록 설계됐다. 사용자의 특성을 잘못 해석한 경우와 주변 환경을 제대로 탐색하지 못한 경우, 실제로 수행할 수 없는 행동을 계획한 경우 등을 구분해 평가한다. 이를 통해 연구진은 AI 모델의 성능 개선이 필요한 영역을 구체적으로 파악하고, 사용자 맞춤형 행동계획 기술을 고도화할 수 있을 것으로 기대하고 있다.
이번 연구는 로봇 AI의 평가 기준을 '주어진 작업을 수행할 수 있는가'에서 '특정 사용자에게 적절한 행동을 수행하는가'로 확장했다는 점에서 의미가 있다. 특히 고령자 돌봄이나 가정 내 생활 지원처럼 사용자별 건강 상태와 생활 습관, 선호도 등을 고려해야 하는 서비스 분야에서는 작업의 물리적 성공 여부만으로 로봇의 유용성과 안전성을 판단하기 어렵다.
ETRI는 트리니티의 코드와 데이터를 공개해 다른 연구진도 사용자 중심 로봇 AI 평가에 활용할 수 있도록 했다. 향후에는 현재 음식과 음료를 중심으로 구성된 평가 대상을 청소와 정리, 안전 등 다양한 생활영역으로 확대하고, 시간에 따라 달라지는 사용자의 요구까지 반영할 계획이다.
김도형 ETRI 소셜로보틱스연구실장은 "기존 로봇 AI 연구가 주변 환경을 이해하고 주어진 작업을 정확하게 수행하는 데 집중했다면, 트리니티는 로봇의 행동이 서비스를 받는 사람에게도 적절한지를 함께 평가한다는 데 의미가 있다"고 설명했다.
이어 "앞으로 로봇이 사람마다 다른 특성과 요구를 이해하고 함께 생활할 수 있는 지능으로 발전하는 데 필요한 연구 기반이 될 것으로 기대한다"고 밝혔다.
이번 기술은 실제 로봇의 물리적 작업 성능을 검증했다기보다, 가상 환경에서 AI 에이전트가 사용자 특성을 반영해 적절한 작업을 계획하는 능력을 평가하는 벤치마크라는 점에서 구분할 필요가 있다. 향후 실제 생활환경과 로봇 하드웨어를 활용한 검증으로 이어질지가 주목된다.
한편, 이번 연구는 ETRI의 김형민, 전호범, 김도형, 장진혁, 장민수 연구진이 수행했다. 연구 결과는 지난달 헝가리 부다페스트에서 개최된 자연어처리 분야 국제학술대회 'EMNLP 2026'의 논문집 '파인딩스(Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EMNLP 2026)'에 '트리니티: 체화된 작업계획을 위한 인간·에이전트·환경 정렬(Trinity: Human–Agent–Environment Alignment for Embodied Task Planning-다운)'란 제목으로 발표됐다.

[TITLE] New SignDrop Zero-Day Exploit Turns Windows CLFS Security Feature Into SYSTEM Access
[TITLE_KO] 새로운 SignDrop 제로데이 익스플로잇, 윈도 CLFS 보안 기능을 SYSTEM 권한 획득에 악용
[LINK] https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/new-signdrop-zero-day-exploit-turns.html
[PUBLISHED] 2026-11-13 02:50 KST
[SOURCE] The Hacker News
[TOPIC] 개발, 보안
[SCORE] 7
[REASON] 윈도 로그 인증 기능을 역이용한 로컬 권한 상승 PoC 대응 필요
[FIELD] 보안
[BODY]
Public exploit code released earlier this week claims that a local Windows user can gain SYSTEM privileges through the Common Log File System driver.
The proof of concept, called SignDrop, targets a log-authentication feature added to the driver last year. It abuses how clfs.sys creates companion files when a privileged process opens a prepared log, using path redirection to take control of the operation. Microsoft has not confirmed the claimed flaw, assigned a CVE, or released a fix.
The researcher behind the exploit, who publishes under the name MSNightmare, released the code and a detailed writeup on GitHub on Tuesday, the same day the company published its November security updates.
CLFS is a logging subsystem built into Windows. Its driver has been a frequent target for privilege escalation: 10 CLFS flaws appear in CISA's catalog of vulnerabilities known to have been exploited in attacks, including one used in ransomware campaigns in April 2025.
To reduce that exposure, Microsoft added an authentication check to CLFS log files in updates for Windows 11 25H2, 26H1, and Windows Server 2025 shipped in October 2025. The check attaches a hash-based message authentication code, built from log data and a key unique to each machine.
The driver then parses only log files it wrote. A companion .cnpf file stores recovery data beside each log and is created with the same security attributes as the original, according to the company's logfile authentication page.
SignDrop targets the creation of that companion file. According to the proof-of-concept code, a standard user prepares a CLFS log and container with paths the user controls. When a privileged process later opens that log, the driver creates the .cnpf file in the privileged process's security context rather than the user's.
The exploit uses object manager symbolic links to redirect that file creation to a location the attacker chooses. In the published code, the privileged process is the Defender antimalware service, triggered through its update interface.
"Everything is done properly by Microsoft here but the logic allows abuse," the researcher wrote.
Because the .cnpf file inherits the original log's permissions, the result is a file created as SYSTEM that the standard user can write to. The researcher says that can be chained into a SYSTEM shell.
The exploit was tested on Windows 11 26H2, a Windows 11 26H2 Experimental build, and Windows Server 2025 Experimental, according to the repository. Systems are affected when the CLFS authentication registry mode is set to 0 (enforced) or 1 (learning). Systems older than 90 days typically move from learning to enforcement automatically, so the default configuration on established installations is the one the exploit targets.
A registry value of 2 or 3 means the mitigation is disabled. That takes the system outside the claimed attack surface, but the researcher and the driver's own documentation both warn against it: disabling lets CLFS parse tampered log files, reopening the memory corruption flaws the protection was built to prevent.
Independent Test
Sungyup Nam, head of research at Korean security firm 78researchlab, reported in a published assessment today that researcher Heewon Song of the firm's PurpleHound offensive content team tested the exploit and found that it works. Song assessed that it does not run reliably enough for operational use, Nam wrote.
Defender must be enabled for the published chain to succeed. Running the exploit changes every UAC prompt to open a standard user shell and a SYSTEM shell, blocking other actions and making the attack noticeable to anyone at the machine.
The repository claims the technique can work against any antimalware engine whose process runs with elevated privileges. That broader claim has not been tested.
What Defenders Can Do
No CVE, advisory, or patch for SignDrop appeared in the November security updates.

[TITLE] AMD, 양자컴퓨터 오류 수정에 GPU·SW 투입…엔비디아와 경쟁
[LINK] https://www.aitimes.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=217381
[PUBLISHED] 2026-11-13 16:34 KST
[SOURCE] AI타임스
[TOPIC] 신기술
[SCORE] 6
[REASON] 양자 컴퓨터 실시간 오류 수정 가속 기술 경쟁 심화
[FIELD] 신기술
[BODY]
AMD가 양자컴퓨팅 기업 사이퀀텀(PsiQuantum)에 지분 투자를 추진하고, 양자컴퓨터의 오류를 실시간으로 탐지·수정하는 데 자사 CPU·GPU와 소프트웨어를 활용하기로 했다. 지난해부터 양자컴퓨팅 인프라 사업을 강화해 온 엔비디아와 관련 기술을 둘러싼 경쟁도 본격화할 전망이다.
AMD와 사이퀀텀은 12일(현지시간) 공동 발표를 통해 지분 투자 계획과 기술 협력 확대를 공개했다. 구체적인 투자 금액은 밝히지 않았다.
이번 협력의 핵심은 대규모 양자컴퓨터의 실시간 오류 수정 기술이다. 양자컴퓨터는 계산 과정에서 외부 환경의 영향 등으로 오류가 발생하기 쉬워 이를 신속하게 감지하고 수정하는 기술이 필수적이다. 양사는 AMD의 고성능 컴퓨팅 기술을 활용해 양자컴퓨터에서 발생하는 오류를 빠르게 처리하고 안정적인 연산을 지원하는 시스템을 개발할 계획이다.
사이퀀텀은 빛의 입자인 광자를 큐비트로 사용하는 양자컴퓨팅 기업으로, 100만 큐비트 규모의 오류 내성 양자컴퓨터 개발을 추진하는 것으로 알려져 있다. 기존 반도체 생산공정을 활용하는 실리콘 포토닉스 기술을 바탕으로 미국 시카고와 호주 브리즈번에 대규모 양자컴퓨터 구축을 추진하고 있다. 100만 큐비트는 현재 구현한 성능이 아닌 장기 개발 목표다.
양사는 이미 기술 협력을 진행해 왔다. 사이퀀텀은 AMD의 프로그래머블 반도체인 '버설 FPGA(Versal FPGA)'를 양자컴퓨터 개발에 활용하고 있다. 이번 협력을 통해 AMD의 '에픽(EPYC)' CPU와 '인스팅트(Instinct)' GPU, 'ROCm' 소프트웨어까지 적용 범위를 넓힐 계획이다.
특히 대규모 양자컴퓨터는 수많은 큐비트를 제어하고 오류를 수정하기 위해 방대한 데이터 처리와 연산 능력이 필요하다. AMD의 CPU와 GPU, FPGA를 결합해 이러한 작업을 기존 고성능 컴퓨팅 시스템에서 처리함으로써 양자컴퓨터의 안정성과 확장성을 높이겠다는 구상이다.
AMD는 지난해부터 양자컴퓨팅 분야에서 협력을 강화해 왔다. 2025년 8월 IBM과 양자컴퓨터·AI·고성능컴퓨팅을 결합하는 차세대 시스템 개발에 착수했으며, 지난 5일에는 IBM·오크리지국립연구소와 GPU를 활용한 양자 오류 수정 연구 플랫폼을 공개했다. 이번 사이퀀텀 투자는 이러한 협력을 광자 양자컴퓨팅으로 확대하는 움직임이다.
양사는 양자컴퓨팅과 고성능 컴퓨팅(HPC), AI를 결합한 응용 기술도 개발한다. 이미 AMD GPU를 활용한 대규모 시뮬레이션에서 항암 신약 개발과 관련된 초기 성과를 확보했다고 밝혔다.
사이퀀텀의 신약 연구 기술을 AMD GPU에서 구동해 항암제 개발에 활용되는 공유결합 억제제(covalent inhibitor)의 작용을 시뮬레이션하는 과정에서 기존 방식보다 정확한 결과를 얻었다는 설명이다. 이는 양자컴퓨터가 아닌 기존 GPU를 활용한 연구 성과다.
양사는 사이퀀텀이 핵심 입주기업으로 참여하는 미국 시카고의 일리노이 양자·마이크로전자공학 파크(IQMP)에서도 협력을 확대하는 방안을 검토하고 있다.
한편, 엔비디아도 지난해부터 양자컴퓨팅 인프라 개발을 본격화했다. 2025년 3월 양자컴퓨팅 연구센터 설립을 발표하고, 같은 해 10월에는 양자 프로세서와 GPU를 연결해 실시간 오류 수정을 지원하는 'NVQLink'를 공개했다.
AMD와 엔비디아 모두 양자 프로세서 자체보다 이를 제어하고 오류를 수정하는 기존 컴퓨팅 인프라에 집중하고 있다. 양자컴퓨터 상용화에 필요한 CPU·GPU와 제어 시스템을 둘러싸고 두 반도체 기업의 사업 영역이 겹치기 시작한 것이다.
